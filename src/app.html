Key what the colours and marks mean
Lines: colour is the job
Green soaks up sunlight
Cyan gathers food bits it touches and nibbles plants
Red bites live prey and corpses
Blue armour, blocks bites and stings, softens spit
Yellow swims fast, costs energy (other lines only wriggle slowly)
Purple poison: biting it poisons the biter, and its pack learns to stay away
White stinger: pins and drains on touch; a hunter stung by its prey backs off
Organs: shape is the job
Eye sees food, prey, danger and mates; eyes sharper than the other side's win the sight race
Fat store holds more energy, burns slowly in winter, slows you
Brood sac breeds sooner, twins, most of all when the dish is thin
Spitter shoots globs that slow prey; a herd can't shield its members from them
Brain smarter moves: aim ahead, ambush, flank, drive, mob, flee the right way, remember danger, stash kills
Nose smells things in the dark (further at night), in the weeds and far-off corpses, but only roughly where
Size is strength
Thicker lines and bigger organs do their job harder, but cost more energy and weight
Living together
Social creatures herd with relatives for protection, or hunt in packs that share a target
Queen (ringed) of a hive: she lays collectors (dark dot), who bring food home, and soldiers (red dot), who guard her; more soldiers when the hive is attacked
Instincts: rings and marks
Blue rim armoured all over: bites do up to half damage, and biting it hurts
Purple dashes the poison look; some are only mimics, which fools hunters without a brain
Lure a hunter with a glowing speck that grazers mistake for food
Thorny a plant whose thorns prick grazers that nibble it
Shrub a tough plant: grows slowly, lives long, hard to eat
Hibernating asleep through the cold, using almost nothing
Grey specks sick: catches it in crowds, slower and hungrier for a while
Small ring an egg waiting for spring

Hidden instincts too: warm or cold-blooded, active by day or night, camouflage (faint), poison tolerance, caring for young, filter feeding (sieves food bits through its whole body), darting (escape bursts) and raiding (hunters drawn to hives). Hover a creature to see them.

The dish
Warm vent puffs out food bits and keeps the cold off nearby
Rock nothing swims through it; run the wrong way and you're cornered
Weed bed hides whoever is inside from sight, but shades out sun and slows you
Food bit drifts in; dead bodies break down into more

Every part costs upkeep, and each extra part costs a bit more than the last. Complex creatures live longer and can do more, but only if they feed themselves. Relatives breed when they meet. Eating can pass the prey's genes to the eater's children.
